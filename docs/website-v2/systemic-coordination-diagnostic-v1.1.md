# Systemic Coordination Diagnostic v1.1

**Status:** Live experimental specification  
**Provider:** alexsotodigital  
**Canonical language:** English

---

## 1. Purpose

The Systemic Coordination Diagnostic helps a team answer:

1. **Where does work appear to be getting stuck?**
2. **How consequential is the problem?**
3. **Can the organization plausibly address it internally?**
4. **What is the smallest useful next step?**

A legitimate result may be:

- no intervention;
- self-guided improvement;
- internal management action;
- targeted coordination intervention;
- broader organizational strategy/design;
- founding-agreement work;
- another specialist;
- not ready yet;
- insufficient information.

The diagnostic should provide value even if the respondent never hires alexsotodigital.

---

## 2. Core principles

### Diagnose mechanisms, not labels

Do not automatically translate:

- slow decisions → unclear roles;
- missed commitments → weak accountability;
- conflict → bad culture;
- growth → more process;
- communication complaints → facilitation.

Use:

**symptom → mechanism → consequence → self-correction → readiness → next action**

### Work from concrete examples

At least one real situation should anchor the assessment.

### Do not assume external intervention

The tool must be capable of saying:

> **Try this internally first.**

### Avoid false precision

Internal scores support routing.

Do not show an overall organizational health score.

### Preserve uncertainty

Prefer:

> “Your answers suggest…”

over:

> “Your organization has…”

---

## 3. Core diagnostic dimensions

1. Authority
2. Accountability
3. Information / Context
4. Interfaces
5. Capacity
6. Power / Relationships
7. Adaptation

Conditional:

8. Founding Agreements

---

## 4. Operational definitions

| Dimension | Question underneath it |
|---|---|
| Authority | Is it clear who can actually decide, and is that authority respected? |
| Accountability | Do commitments have meaningful ownership, observable outcomes and review? |
| Information / Context | Does relevant information reach the people who need it in time? |
| Interfaces | Do dependencies and handoffs work reliably? |
| Capacity | Are commitments compatible with actual resources and capabilities? |
| Power / Relationships | Can people disagree or surface bad news without unreasonable cost? |
| Adaptation | When something is known not to work, does behavior change? |
| Founding Agreements | Are key expectations among founders sufficiently explicit? |

---

## 5. Intended respondent

v1.1 assumes one respondent describing one organizational unit.

Potential respondents:

- founder / partner;
- executive;
- operations leader;
- People / HR;
- manager;
- individual contributor;
- internal organizational practitioner;
- board/governance member.

Results represent one vantage point, not objective organizational truth.

Response scale Q9–Q29:

- Not at all
- Rarely
- Sometimes
- Often
- Very much

`0–4`

### Authority

**Q9.** People generally know who has authority to make recurring decisions.

Positive.

**Q10.** Decisions are sometimes reopened or overridden by people outside the role that was supposed to own them.

Negative.

**Q11.** Important work depends on approval from one or a few key people even when others are formally responsible for it.

Negative.

### Accountability

**Q12.** Important agreements usually leave the conversation with a clear owner, expected outcome and review point.

Positive.

**Q13.** The team repeatedly agrees on things that later fail to happen.

Negative.

**Q14.** People are sometimes held responsible for results they do not have enough authority or influence to produce.

Negative.

### Information / Context

**Q15.** The people making important decisions usually have the context they need.

Positive.

**Q16.** Important information from execution reaches the people who can change priorities, plans or strategy.

Positive.

**Q17.** Work is regularly slowed because information arrives late, incomplete or fragmented.

Negative.

### Interfaces

**Q18.** When work passes from one person or team to another, expectations and responsibilities are generally clear.

Positive.

**Q19.** Different teams may perform well independently while the overall work still breaks down between them.

Negative.

**Q20.** Dependencies and escalation paths across teams are explicit enough for people to know what to do when something gets stuck.

Positive.

### Capacity

**Q21.** Commitments generally reflect the team's actual time, skills and resources.

Positive.

**Q22.** The organization regularly commits to more than the responsible people can realistically deliver.

Negative.

**Q23.** Coordination problems persist partly because key people are overloaded.

Negative.

### Power / Relationships

**Q24.** People can challenge a decision or surface bad news without unreasonable personal or political cost.

Positive.

**Q25.** Some important problems are difficult to discuss openly because of hierarchy, status, politics or relationships.

Negative.

**Q26.** Disagreement can usually be resolved without relying on hidden vetoes or informal power.

Positive.

### Adaptation

**Q27.** When the team identifies something that is not working, it usually changes something observable afterward.

Positive.

**Q28.** The same organizational problems are discussed repeatedly without meaningful changes in how the team works.

Negative.

**Q29.** When the team changes a process or agreement, it later checks whether the change worked.

Positive.

# Scoring

## Mechanism responses

Negative/friction statements:

- Not at all = 0
- Rarely = 1
- Sometimes = 2
- Often = 3
- Very much = 4

Positive statements:

`friction = 4 - response`

Dimension score:

`mean(items)`

Interpretation:

| Score | Level |
|---:|---|
| 0–0.99 | Low friction |
| 1–1.99 | Some friction |
| 2–2.99 | Material friction |
| 3–4 | High friction |

Raw numbers remain internal.

---

# Pattern logic

## Single primary pattern

Use when:

- highest dimension ≥ 2;
- difference from second-highest ≥ `0.35`.

## Combined pattern

If two top material/high dimensions differ by less than `0.35`, present a combined pattern where conceptually meaningful.

Examples:

- Authority + Accountability
- Information + Interfaces
- Power + Adaptation

## Systemic / multi-mechanism pattern

Use when:

- three or more dimensions are Material/High;
- no single mechanism clearly dominates.

This should increase probability of broader organizational-design routing.

---

# Composite patterns

Underlying dimension scores remain canonical.

Composite patterns improve interpretation.

### Founder dependency

Possible signals:

- Authority friction
- Information concentration
- Capacity/dependency
- Excess escalation to one/few people

### Agreement without accountability

- Accountability friction
- Adaptation friction
- Repeated commitments without behavioral change

### Strategy–execution disconnect

- Information friction
- Interface friction
- Adaptation friction

### False delegation

- Formal clarity appears high
- Actual authority friction high
- Decisions reopened/overridden

### Coordination overload

- Capacity friction
- Interface friction
- Growth/change context

### Known problem / blocked change

- Adaptation friction
- Power friction
- Material/high consequence
- Low readiness

Composite patterns must not override deterministic dimension scores.

# Severity

Do not average frequency and consequence.

Use:

| Consequence ↓ / Frequency → | Rare | Monthly | Several/month | Weekly | Daily |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minor | Low | Low | Low | Moderate | Moderate |
| Manageable | Low | Low | Moderate | Moderate | Material |
| Meaningful friction | Moderate | Moderate | Material | Material | High |
| Material consequence | Material | Material | Material | High | High |
| Serious risk | High | High | High | High | High |

Q32–Q33 provide qualitative interpretation only.

# Self-correction capacity

Question:

> **Can this system repair itself?**

Use primarily:

- Adaptation dimension;
- Q34;
- Q35.

Do not use sponsor authority or willingness to hire external support.

Interpret:

- Strong
- Moderate
- Weak

---

# Readiness

Question:

> **Could meaningful organizational change actually be implemented now?**

Use:

- Q36 authority;
- Q37 sponsor participation;
- Q38 willingness;
- Q39 capacity;
- Q40 leadership openness.

Encode strongest → weakest as 4 → 0.

`readiness = mean(Q36–Q40)`

Bands:

- 3–4 High
- 2–2.99 Moderate
- 1–1.99 Low
- 0–0.99 Very low

Critical variables:

- Q36 authority;
- Q40 leadership openness.

If either is at the lowest level, normally do not recommend a standard organizational intervention.

---

# Respondent vantage point

Use Q1 to contextualize confidence.

Examples:

A founder may have better visibility into:

- formal authority;
- strategic constraints.

But less visibility into:

- psychological safety;
- whether staff feel able to challenge decisions.

An individual contributor may have strong visibility into:

- interfaces;
- execution;
- informal power.

But less visibility into:

- executive constraints;
- financial resources;
- board-level authority.

Do not invalidate perspectives.

Do not treat one perspective as the complete system.

---

# Contradiction logic

Contradictions reduce confidence.

They do not automatically change friction scores.

Examples:

### Authority contradiction

Q9 indicates very high clarity while Q8 indicates formal authority was overridden.

Flag:

`authorityContradiction = true`

### Adaptation contradiction

Q27 indicates strong adaptation while Q28 indicates repeated unresolved problems.

Flag:

`adaptationContradiction = true`

### Open-text contradiction

If open responses visibly contradict structured answers, the UI may acknowledge uncertainty.

Do not send open text to an LLM or silently overwrite deterministic scores.

---

# Confidence

## High

- concrete example provided;
- structured responses substantially complete;
- few contradictions;
- reasonable respondent visibility.

## Medium

- broadly sufficient evidence;
- some uncertainty;
- partial vantage point.

## Low

- example missing/vague;
- many unsure answers;
- several contradictions;
- recommendation depends heavily on inaccessible information.

Low confidence should favor clarification over strong recommendations.

# Routing precedence

Evaluate in this order.

## 1. Scope / safety review

If Q41 flags concern:

→ **Specialist / human review**

Stop normal routing.

## 2. Insufficient information

If confidence Low:

→ **Insufficient information**

## 3. No meaningful friction

If:

- mechanisms mostly Low/Some;
- severity Low;
- self-correction Strong/Moderate;

→ **No external intervention needed**

## 4. Not ready

If:

- severity Material/High;
- but authority, willingness or leadership openness are Low/Very Low;

→ **Not ready yet**

## 5. Founding Agreements

If module active and founding friction Material/High:

→ **Founding Agreements route**

## 6. Primarily capacity / internal management

If:

- Capacity High;
- structural mechanisms mostly Low/Some;
- power/adaptation do not suggest deeper blockage;

→ **Internal management / capacity action**

## 7. Self-guided improvement

If:

- one/two dimensions Some/Material;
- severity Low/Moderate;
- self-correction Strong/Moderate;
- readiness sufficient;

→ **Self-guided improvement**

## 8. Targeted Coordination Intervention

If:

- one/two mechanisms Material/High;
- severity Material/High;
- readiness Moderate/High;
- adequate authority exists;

→ **Targeted Coordination Intervention**

Potential paid route:

**Coordination Reset**

## 9. Broader Organizational Strategy & Design

If:

- three+ mechanisms materially interact;
- or major growth/restructuring/strategy transition is central;
- severity Material/High;
- readiness sufficient;

→ **Broader Organizational Strategy & Design**

# Route definitions

### No external intervention needed

Normal tensions exist but the system appears reasonably capable of correcting them internally.

Provide a preventive practice.

No sales CTA required.

### Self-guided improvement

The issue appears bounded enough to try internally first.

Provide a mechanism-specific exercise.

Optional secondary CTA if the problem persists.

### Internal management / capacity action

Primary constraint appears to be resources/prioritization rather than coordination design.

Possible actions:

- reduce commitments;
- reprioritize;
- reallocate resources;
- stop work;
- hire;
- renegotiate scope.

Do not frame overwork as an accountability failure.

### Targeted Coordination Intervention

Problem appears sufficiently bounded to work on directly but consequential enough that structured outside support may accelerate progress.

Potential route:

**Coordination Reset**

### Broader Organizational Strategy & Design

Multiple mechanisms appear interconnected.

Fixing one role, process or meeting is unlikely to be enough.

### Founding Agreements

Main tensions appear connected to insufficiently explicit expectations among founders/partners.

### Not ready yet

Problem appears real but authority, willingness or capacity for meaningful change is insufficient.

Provide a readiness action.

### Specialist / human review

Some aspects may fall outside the appropriate scope of organizational coordination work.

Do not state legal, clinical or safeguarding conclusions.

### Insufficient information

Not enough consistent evidence for a responsible recommendation.

Offer clarification or exploration call.

# Self-guided practices

## Authority — Decision Reality Map

Choose five recurring decisions.

Identify:

- formal decision owner;
- actual decision owner;
- required consultation;
- override authority;
- escalation path.

Compare the formal and real systems.

## Accountability — Agreement-to-Action Check

Take one important agreement.

Make explicit:

**Owner → observable outcome → review point → response if reality changes**

## Information / Context — Decision Context Check

Choose one poor/delayed decision.

Ask:

- What information was required?
- Who had it?
- Who needed it?
- When did it arrive?
- Where did the signal stop?

## Interfaces — Handoff Map

Choose one recurring dependency.

Define:

- what enters;
- what “ready” means;
- who accepts;
- required context;
- feedback route;
- escalation route.

## Capacity — Commitment Reality Check

For each major commitment:

- owner;
- required capacity;
- actual capacity;
- trade-offs;
- what should stop if this remains a priority.

## Power / Relationships — Dissent Test

Ask:

> Which important decision would be hardest to challenge openly right now?

Then:

- Why?
- What would someone risk?
- Is there a legitimate route for dissent?
- Who can change the decision?

## Adaptation — Repeated Problem Review

Take one issue discussed at least three times.

Map:

**signal → conversation → decision → behavior change → review**

Find where the loop stops.

## Founding Agreements — Founder Stress Test

Discuss:

- contribution;
- money;
- ownership;
- authority;
- risk;
- values;
- changes in commitment;
- exit.
